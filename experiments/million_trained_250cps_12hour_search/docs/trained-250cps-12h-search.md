Twelve-hour session completed: 2026-09-14, 02:24:58–14:25 UTC.
Target: one million lifetime cookies on game 2.031, 250 clicks/s, 0.4 seconds
per errand, 0.1 seconds per shop action, fixed ×10 buying, no selling.

### What helped

- **Represent baskets without enumerating equivalent click orders.** Under
  fixed ×10 without selling, canonical ordering removes large redundant
  families. The [ordering proof](x10-partial-purchase-order.md) states its scope.
- **Skip infeasible intermediate quantities while growing baskets.** Some
  useful baskets cannot be reached by accepting only valid one-unit children.
  Advancing to the next feasible quantity recovered all 263,509 valid baskets
  in a 500-state, three-building audit; ordinary growth missed 585.
- **Rank some candidates by their continuation.** Future inventory estimates
  and complete greedy continuations made upgrade-heavy errands easier to find.
  A flat upgrade bonus alone was inconsistent. Keep some candidates selected
  by the immediate score as well.
- **Refine complete routes cheaply.** Quantity, order and errand-boundary changes
  found gains that wider beams missed. Cached exact evaluation, partition
  parent links and native worker threads made these searches much cheaper.
- **Remove an obstructive price horizon.** The winning opening contains an
  errand costing 16.84 times the cookies produced at its starting state; the
  previous horizon of 16 excludes it.

### What did not reliably help

Wider queues, larger upgrade bonuses and more diverse candidate pools all
increase the search space, but did not consistently improve complete routes.
Worker prefetch increased throughput yet produced worse routes in the timed
comparison. It is optional and remains off by default. A large upgrade-factor
cache gave too little benefit to keep.

The Python routers retain their public behavior apart from a safe shortcut
that avoids repeated partial purchases of the same building in a no-selling
×10 errand. New planners and heuristics live in
[`scripts/`](../scripts/).
Their native simulator is deliberately restricted to this profile; every saved
candidate is checked by the existing Python simulator.

The latest fresh build passed **73 repository tests and 21 experimental tests**.
Additional differential audits cover the native simulator, canonical orders,
partition optimizations and resumable quantity scans. Results are best-found
under the continuous simulator, not a proof of global optimality or an observed
in-game speedrun. The comparison also depends on the exact action delays:
some nearly tied alternatives become faster if those delays decrease.

Compact measurements, full route snapshots and export provenance are preserved
in [the results JSON](trained-250cps-search-results.json). The detailed research
log below includes negative results and qualifications; bulky temporary logs
and binary snapshots remain in `/tmp/ccsr-trained-12h-20260914/`.

## Detailed research log

## Questions and measurements

- Can the generator recover known good errands with fewer returned candidates?
- Do upgrade bonuses improve complete routes, rather than just local rankings?
- Does reserving room for different upgrade/building strategies beat simply
  increasing the queue size?
- Does allowing locally worse parents to be extended uncover useful errands?
- Which changes remain helpful on newly discovered routes and independent seeds?

Track candidate coverage, generator time, complete-route time, state expansions,
and whether a search actually improves its supplied ruler. Preserve negative
results and separate heuristic choices from safe equivalence/dominance rules.

## Initial observations

The existing inner search stops when its best remaining parent scores worse
than the worst returned errand. Child scores need not be monotone: appending
an upgrade or sharing an errand's fixed delay can improve the score. This
stopping rule therefore deserves an ablation before increasing search budgets.

The sections below record the measurements in the order they were made.

## Full-batch order normalization under investigation

With no selling, full ×10 purchases can often move before partial purchases
without changing the recorded quantities. Upgrades can also move into that
prefix when their building requirements are already met by its full batches.
Only the remaining partial purchases retain ordering distinctions. The
experimental generator normalizes this prefix and keeps partial-click order;
a simulator comparison checks that the transformation preserves the endpoint.
This is deliberately separate from heuristic strategy bucketing, which may
exclude useful candidates and cannot claim exact equivalence.

## Cached evaluation

The experimental cached evaluator matched stock validity, Purchase records,

across 38 states, including banks created by extra waiting. Of these,
7,065 were valid. The evaluator itself was 4.19× faster.
A separate 19-state whole-generator comparison produced identical results
and measured 2.30× speedup. This implementation is restricted to fixed ×10
without selling and falls back to stock evaluation outside that scope.

The full-core normalization check considered 46,550 candidates; 289 were
valid, and 47 changed order while preserving the endpoint. No game or shop
semantics were changed. Timings were measured under concurrent search load.

## A better opening: 198.146636 seconds

The cooler native search found a replay-verified **198.146636-second (3:18.15)**
route. It starts with one Cursor, then Reinforced index finger, then Carpal
tunnel prevention cream, each in its own errand. Only afterward does it buy a
full batch of ten Cursors with Ambidextrous. The previous best bought ten
Cursors with Reinforced index finger as its opening errand.

This buys the first click multipliers earlier despite paying for one extra
errand. A later partial Cursor purchase compensates for the extra initial
Cursor, so the route does not carry a permanently larger Cursor inventory.
The new route has 20 errands and 28 shop actions. Its improvement over the
session's starting incumbent is 0.944075 seconds.

The opening also illustrates a limitation of witness coverage as a metric:
two-step lookahead had demoted the old opening, which looked like a coverage
regression, but it promotes the newly useful one-Cursor opening. Immediate
repayment scoring strongly undervalues that Cursor because its immediate
production gain is tiny; its value is unlocking the next upgrade cheaply.
A bonus applied only to upgrade-containing errands cannot address this case.

The hotter native schedule (temperature 3→0.03, 1,500-trial restarts) did not
improve the incumbent after more than 20 million attempts. A cooler schedule
(0.3→0.001, 50,000-trial restarts), local-biased mutations, crossover, and
additional archived seed orders found the new opening in about 95 seconds.
These changes were bundled, so this trial does not identify their individual
causal contributions. Separate comparisons remain necessary.

The updated 20-errand best route is a harder and more relevant witness than

The real states, affordability checks, and replay remain unchanged. Taking

Adding Thousand fingers hypothetically at every stage overvalues a different

### Correcting an outer-queue bias

A newly instrumented two-minute beam with ordering scalar 1.01, suffix-rollout
weight 0.5, and ruler-prefix seeds expanded 23,124 states: none before 250,000
lifetime cookies,3 between 250 k–500 k,2,204 between 500 k–750 k,14,707 between 750 k–900 k,
and 6,210 after 900 k. It was effectively refining the ending, despite having all
ruler prefixes in its queue. This explains why very large neighbor totals
were not evidence of broad exploration of the opening or middle game.

The 1.01 ordering scalar had been chosen separately from the 0.94 pace-pruning
scalar. With a good incumbent and prefix seeding, it makes late prefixes
cheaper to pop. The previous 15 minute score trials therefore have a confound:
no improvement does not demonstrate that an early-game scoring variation is
unhelpful. The remaining trials in those sequences were interrupted and are
being replaced by queue-order and stage-coverage comparisons. This is a
search-scheduling issue, not an error in replayed route times.

The future-score greedy route (Plastic-mouse projection plus two-step weight
0.5) finished 202.983943 seconds, versus 205.397473 for the existing stock greedy.
Independent local refinement of that order reached 198.146636 again in roughly
30 seconds. The better greedy provides a useful seed without archived routes,
but has not yet improved the best refined finish.


### Balanced scheduling and bounded memory

The experimental native beam now has optional scheduling across ten lifetime
cookie ranges. It expands the least-served nonempty range, taking the best
priority within that range. The exact state-dominance rule is unchanged.
In a 90 second probe, nine populated ranges each received about 510 expansions;
the tiny opening range had 16. This prevents both late-game starvation of
early decisions and the reverse bias from an aggressively low ordering scalar.

When trimming a balanced frontier, each nonempty range first receives an equal
share of the queue capacity; unused capacity is filled by global priority.
Optional node compaction retains queued states, harvested candidates, and all
of their ancestors. It rebuilds route indices and the dominance table from
those retained nodes. Forgotten explored states may be visited again, trading
some repeated work for bounded memory. The saved incumbent route is independent
of node indices and remains available throughout.

A small-memory stress run performed 45 compactions in 8.84 seconds before its tiny
frontier exhausted. All 16 saved near-miss routes and the final incumbent still
matched authoritative Python replay. This tests ancestry preservation, not
search quality at such a small memory limit.


### Three-step greedy improvement

The native lookahead can now evaluate two subsequent errands after a candidate.
It retains a few immediately strong second steps plus every feasible upgrade
branch, so a weak intermediate upgrade is not discarded before its payoff can
be seen. The candidate state is never modified by this scoring calculation.
With a 3,000 candidate scoring pool, full lookahead weight, four ordinary
continuation branches, and Plastic-mouse projection for queue expansion, a
complete greedy route finished 201.485756 seconds in about 6 seconds of search.
That is 3.911717 seconds faster than the existing 205.397473 second stock greedy.
A smaller 500 candidate pool gave 202.011795 without projection and 202.077538 with
projection; a half-weight variant regressed to 207.660424.

Both tested full-weight three-step variants match 17/20 next states of the best
refined route, versus 19/20 for projection alone. Better witness coverage does
not imply a better greedy route. These are useful distinct measurements, and
the improved greedy is being refined independently as another starting order.


### Quantity-based diversity: inconclusive so far

Splitting strategy families by log 2 quantity ranges or groups of five did not
improve the small witness audit. With width 160, inner 400,1,000 expansions, and
Plastic-mouse projection, the best tested log 2 quota gave 18/20 matches versus
19/20 without a diversity quota. Several stronger quotas dropped to 11–12/20.
These results show that aggressive diversity can discard useful neighboring
quantities as well as redundant-looking alternatives. The options remain
experimental; they are not a default or a proven dominance rule.

A separate 15 minute beam is testing actual breadth: up to 2,500 returned errands
per state, inner queue 2,000,5,000 inner expansions, prospective Plastic-mouse
scoring, and balanced outer scheduling. This tests the cost of exposing more
candidates directly, instead of assuming the narrower heuristic has already
retained every useful choice.


### An exact partial-purchase ordering reduction

A new optional normalization sorts partial purchases by decreasing total price
of the requested quantity plus one more building. An adjacent-exchange proof
shows that, once independent full batches and ready upgrades are placed first,
this order is feasible whenever any order of the same distinct-building partial
purchases is feasible. It preserves the endpoint under the grouped-errand model.
The proof and conditions are in[x10-partial-purchase-order.md](x10-partial-purchase-order.md).

An exhaustive small-basket check compared 221,120 permutations across 5,000 sampled
baskets against Python replay, including 2,773 cases with positive starting
banks. All checks passed. The normalization recovered 649 initially invalid
sequences and found 438 baskets with multiple valid orders. It may also let the
generator grow a useful basket from a cheaper parent, by inserting a newly
added expensive partial purchase in its feasible position instead of appending
it. The first witness audit remains 19/20 with the prospective-mouse score;
complete beam and partition-search trials are now testing its practical value.


### Growing quantities without adding clicks

The next generator variant increments an existing partial building quantity,
turning 9 into 10 without adding a shop click. A full batch can also be added as
a second growth choice. Once partial purchases have a canonical feasible order,
this permits five or ten building-growth edges per expansion instead of fifty
possible appended shop actions. Upgrade and prerequisite macros remain available.

At width 160/inner 400/1,000 expansions, both reduced variants retained 19/20 witness
states with prospective-mouse scoring. The one-at-a-time version took 0.83 seconds
for the 20 state audit versus 1.55 secondsfor the action-based partial-normalizing
version; these wall measurements include replay overhead and concurrent work.
A separate CPU-time benchmark at width 2,500 is testing the gain more directly.
The reduced variants gave 203.777317 second greedy routes. Combining the full-batch
jump variant with three-step lookahead gave 202.335763, slower than the current
best 201.485756 greedy, so neither search speed nor witness coverage alone settles
which variant to use for large beams.


### Measured speed gains and preserved outputs

At width 2,500/inner 2,000/5,000 expansions, direct native calls retained all 20
witness errands with both action-based and quantity-based growth. Across the
20 states, CPU time was 5.1245 secondsfor action growth versus 1.2399 secondsfor
unit-quantity growth:4.13× faster, with 47,471 returned candidates in either case.
Full-batch jumps plus unit growth took 1.7014 seconds. These variants do not
return identical candidate sets, despite matching the witness route.

A separate exact optimization skips a second partial purchase of the same
building in a no-selling errand. The first partial click would already have
been able to afford that later purchase, so such an action sequence is invalid
in the grouped model. Full batches are retained because normalization can move
them before the partial purchase. The native before/after comparison checked
126 state/option combinations byte-for-byte, including all returned scores,
states, and actions. Outputs were identical; action-based generation used about
1.4× less CPU. Quantity-only modes were unchanged, as expected.

The same conservative skip is now in the production Python generator. It is
disabled whenever sales are present and leaves ×1 generation unchanged. Across
63 states from three routes, all 1,260 returned neighbors matched the saved original
implementation exactly. CPU time fell from 8.3951 to 7.1179 seconds, an 18%improvement.
All 73 repository tests passed after this change.

An optional persistent native worker pool preserves batch commitment order.
Two fixed 1,000-expansion comparisons produced identical states, counts, and
route outputs. CPU use fell from 1.6678 to 1.5703 secondsfor 100 inner expansions,
and 12.1959 to 11.5550 secondsfor 1,000 inner expansions: roughly 5–6%. Subsequent
large trials enable this pool explicitly. The existing Python process-pool
implementation is unchanged.


### Handmade-cookie dominance and inventory-target rollouts

An optional exact early-state filter compares only states with identical
inventory, upgrades, bank and lifetime production. If one has no greater age
and at least as many handmade cookies, it dominates the other in this
restricted model: handmade cookies only satisfy minimum upgrade thresholds.
Bank balances are never rounded or merged. The frontier implementation passed
40,000 comparisons against a simple oracle and 549,359 continuation checks.
A search-cost comparison is running; correctness does not imply a useful
speed improvement.

A separate optional rollout aims for each subsequent ruler inventory, buying
only missing buildings and upgrades. This can reuse an investment made by a
different prefix instead of repeating the ruler's original purchases. It is a
feasible completion estimate, not an admissible lower bound. Infeasible
partial-purchase baskets still stop the rollout. Modes test this completion
alone or alongside the original action-list completion. All 11 native
regressions pass, including replay of these rollouts after memory compaction.

The 15-minute three-step beam completed without improving 198.146636 seconds.
The stage-balanced baseline, prospective-mouse, guided, and combined guided
trials also did not improve that incumbent. The combined guided trial expanded
97,498 states and returned 19,132,020 neighbors in 900 seconds. These are
negative results from bounded searches, not evidence of global optimality.


### Strategy diversity and a quantity-growth gap

An archive audit found 1,776 saved near misses but only two upgrade orders;
1,759 used the incumbent order. A new optional archive keeps the best route
per upgrade-order signature, with a wider time allowance. Its hashes are
used only for seed diversity, never for state dominance. A root-only beam is
testing this archive with an eight-second allowance.

Future-building scoring adds the same forecast inventory to virtual copies
of a candidate and its parent. Upgrades can then receive credit for improving
those later buildings. The actual state is unchanged. A cheap greedy with
forecast counts (10, 10, 10, 3, 0) and prospective Plastic mouse finished in
202.046030 seconds, versus 203.777317 for quantity growth with the mouse
projection alone. The forecast variants retained only 16–18 of 20 incumbent
witnesses at width 160, so this is an exploration option, not a clear default.
The existing 201.485756-second three-step greedy remains faster.

An unbounded reachability audit enumerated 263,509 valid three-building
partial baskets across 500 legally constructed ancestor states. Unit-only
growth missed 585 baskets, even without queue pruning. Skipping infeasible
intermediate quantities and stopping at the next feasible quantity reached
all enumerated baskets. Automatic production during a shop pause and surplus
starting bank can make a small partial purchase infeasible while a larger
one is valid. New quantity modes 4 and 5 test this repair, with mode 5 also
allowing a direct full-batch jump. This finite audit is not a completeness
proof for all errands, particularly those containing upgrades.


### Retaining search knowledge across memory compaction

The inventory part of the native state key now uses a lossless bit encoding:
seven bits per building count and sixteen upgrade bits. Bank, lifetime cookies
and handmade cookies retain their exact double representations. This reduced
the key from 48 to 32 bytes. Before subsequent memo changes, 105 generator
comparisons and a 10,001-expansion whole-search comparison produced identical
outputs. Peak memory in that search fell from 88,320 to 85,384 KiB; CPU fell
from 137.60 to 134.74 seconds. The key-size reduction is larger than the
whole-process memory reduction because keys are only part of the storage.

A separate optional change stores the best arrival age independently of a
route-history node. Compaction can then discard old history while remembering
states that were already expanded. States dropped by the bounded queue before
expansion are allowed to be generated again. A stress run survived 47
compactions, exhausted its small queue after 2,451 expansions, and replayed all
32 archived routes successfully. The matched baseline used 2,500 expansions and 48 compactions to exhaust its queue. Both kept the same incumbent; this small stress test shows only a modest reduction in repeated work.

The parameter phase has three streams: root-only beams without suffix
rollouts; incumbent-guided beams varying pruning, ordering, lookahead and
breadth; and fixed-upgrade-order refinement. A seed sweep produced 57 upgrade
orders within 212 seconds by moving, swapping or omitting upgrades and
repartitioning the errands. Each is being given an independent refinement
budget so a promising alternative can be optimized without immediately
changing back to the incumbent order.

root-only width-400 beam, with no ruler-suffix rollouts, independently
constructed a 198.167625-second route in 15 minutes (75,923 expansions).
This is a generated result rather than the fallback ruler's recorded time.

The first wider inventory-guided parameter trial found a small improvement:
**198.144159 seconds**, 2.478 milliseconds faster, with 20 errands and 30
shop actions. It brings Steel-plated rolling pins before Plastic mouse and
delays Cheap hoes until afterward. The fifth errand combines a full Cursor
batch, fourteen Grandmas in two clicks, two Grandma upgrades, and six Farms.
The trial uses width 1,200, inner queue 1,500, 5,000 inner expansions,
first-feasible quantity growth, prospective Plastic mouse, both forms of

The diversity archive produced 192 upgrade orders, 163 beyond the 57-order
local seed sweep. These new orders are queued for short locked-order
refinement after the initial sweep. The initial order sweep already contains
an alternative only 3.144 milliseconds slower than the older incumbent.

An exact partition-result cache preserves the complete fixed-trial annealing
trajectory: at 100,000 trials, all 64 unlocked and all four locked improvements
matched with caching enabled or disabled. CPU use fell by about 14–15%.
The cache compares full action vectors, not just hashes. Its size is bounded
and it clears when full. The next seed sweep enables a 100,000-entry cache.

A reproducible end-to-end recipe generated the 201.485756-second greedy and
refined it to 198.146636 seconds in **9.14 seconds wall time** on this machine
during the ongoing searches. Greedy choices use only the initial game state;
the existing route supplied to that command is a reported comparison, not
a source of purchases. The refinement uses seed 945, width 4, 100,000 trials,
temperature 0.4 to 0.001, 20,000-trial cycles, block mutations and exact caching.
Run `python3 experiments/million_trained_250cps_12hour_search/scripts/run_fast_recipe.py` after building
the native experiments. It independently replays both outputs.

By 07:05 UTC, the best replayed route is **198.140717 seconds**, with 19
errands and 29 shop actions. A clean independent run,
`new_cached_order_005.route`, reproduced this after the first discovery in
an overlapping-controller trial. The overlap affected log/timing provenance,
not the route: 918 recorded route events were recovered and replayed, and the

Wider local generation alone was costly. Fifteen-minute root searches with
no suffix rollouts directly generated 198.167625 seconds at width 400,
198.182160 at width 1,200, and only 210.680776 at width 2,500. The widest run
expanded 8,819 states and never reached the later production stages; the
narrow run expanded 75,923. These are time-limited exploration results,
not evidence that the wider candidate set is intrinsically worse.

A feasible ruler-completion score can select a useful subset from a large
errand pool. Against the older 198.146636-second ruler, the new early errand
ranked 1,048 by local score but 36 by completion. Keeping 80 or 160 candidates
with a quarter reserved for raw-score choices retained all 20 errands of
the then-best witness. In three 3,000-expansion comparisons, all settings
found 198.144159 seconds. Stored nodes fell from 467,565 without selection
to 43,141 with 80 kept and 74,290 with 160 kept; CPU fell from 167.96 to
163.58 and 159.15 seconds respectively. This supports a longer trial, but
it is only one witness and selection can still miss a different strategy.

Two small optimizations improve capacity. Exact state keys now pack the
building counts and upgrade mask while preserving bank, produced cookies
and handmade cookies bit for bit. A fixed-search comparison reduced memory
about 3% and CPU about 2%. Handmade cookies remain capped at the last relevant
unlock threshold, as in the preceding key implementation. An optional bound inside errand generation drops
errands whose end time already reaches the incumbent finish; three repeated
witness passes used 3.60 rather than 4.35 CPU seconds (17.3% less), retaining
all witness errands. Both live in the experimental native implementation.

The root-only forecast-inventory trial independently constructed the current
**198.140717-second** best from the initial game state, without suffix rollouts
or seeded prefixes. It used width 1,200, inner queue 1,500, 5,000 inner
expansions, and future inventory fraction 0.1; 28,749 outer expansions and
28,462,712 returned neighbors in 15 minutes. Its ruler still influenced the
forecast and queue scoring, so this is independent route construction, not
a search uninformed by the incumbent.

Caching feasible completion durations by exact game state, excluding absolute
age, speeds the ruler-based candidate selection. Each worker has a bounded
cache invalidated on ruler changes. A 74,031-query check differed from the
uncached calculation by at most 1.71e-13 seconds. A full 3,000-expansion
comparison produced the same 198.144159-second result and 74,290 stored
nodes, using 150.89 rather than 180.37 CPU seconds (16.3% less). Only ranking
estimates use this cache; actual reported routes still undergo Python replay.

Repartitioning three fixed purchase sequences at widths 1 through 256 did
not change their best times. A systematic pass over the current incumbent
also found no gain among 15,634 distinct edits: one or two changed building
quantities, single deletions/insertions/moves/swaps, and moves of blocks of
two through four actions. This is a bounded local-neighborhood result,
not an optimality proof. Larger coordinated quantity edits are being tested.
